# Lists — the most common block in the language, and the easiest to get wrong.
# The marker states the kind of list. Pick it once and hold it.

## unordered — order does not matter
 · lacquer, bone and gold
 · two triggers, spent only on events
 · one source, thirteen targets
 ↑ a middle dot, not a bullet: • is a hole punched in the line

## ordered — order is the information
 1.  read the philosophy
 2.  lift the palette
 3.  paste one prompt module
 ↑ numbers right-aligned past nine, or 9. and 10. will not line up

## a ladder — order AND precedence
 1  rarity beats taste
 2  meaning beats preference
 3  legibility beats atmosphere
 ↑ no full stop: this is a rank, not a sentence

## checklist — a thing a person is going to do
 ✓ palette resolved
 ✓ contrast proven
 ▫ themes baked
 ▫ site rebuilt
 ↑ ▫ is empty, not ✗: not-yet-done is not failure

## definition — a term and what it means
 token     a raw value with a stated job
 role      the contract you consume — text.body, action.edge
 target    a program the palette is baked for
 ↑ this is a key-value column; see kv.txt if the values grow

## nested — two levels, and there is no third
 canon/
   · palette     tokens, roles, the proof
   · components  the anatomy of a control
 library/
   · web         the drop-in kit
   · themes      thirteen programs
 ↑ a third level means the outline is the document. Split the page instead.

## inline — a list inside a sentence
 kitty, foot, alacritty, ghostty and wezterm
 ↑ commas, and one "and". Not middle dots, which are for pairs, not prose.

## a run of pairs on one line
 stable · v1.2.0 · MIT · 42 tokens

## what a list must not do
 ✗  a marker per line that changes halfway down
 ✗  a one-item list                      that is a sentence
 ✗  a list of full paragraphs            that is prose with bullets stapled on
 ✗  bold on every item                   then bold means nothing

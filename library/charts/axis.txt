# Axes, ticks and legends — the furniture. It should be the quietest thing on
# the chart and still answer "compared to what?".

## tick marks — pick by how much room the axis has
 ├─────┼─────┼─────┤     full: ends and interior ticks
 ╶─────┬─────┬─────╴     open: the data does not stop at the ends
 └─────┴─────┴─────┘     a floor only, when the y-axis is implied
 0     250   500   750   labels under the ticks, never between them

## how many ticks
 4 or 5. Three is a hint, seven is a grid. If you need seven, the reader
 needs a table.

## the y-axis — right-aligned, so the digits line up with each other
  500┤
  250┤
  100┤
    0└──────────────

## a baseline that is not zero — say so, loudly
   80┤                        ⢀⡠⠔⠊
   60┤            ⢀⣀⡠⠤⠒⠉
   40┤⣀⡠⠤⠒⠉
     └────────────────────────────
     ⚠ y starts at 40, not 0
 ↑ a truncated axis doubles every slope. Either start at zero or print this
   line; there is no third option.

## legends — inline beats a key, always
 this year  ▰▰▰▰▰▰▰▰   1.2M
 last year  ▱▱▱▱▱▱     0.9M
 ↑ the label sits on the thing it names. A separate key makes the eye carry
   a colour across the page and back.

 when a key is unavoidable, keep it one line and always in series order:
  ▰ served   ▱ cached   ◆ failed

## units — in the header, once
 p99 (ms)    486      not:   486 ms, 211 ms, 198 ms down the column

## no data — the axis still renders
  100┤
     │            no data for this window
    0└──────────────────────────────────
 ↑ an empty chart that collapses to nothing reads as a bug
